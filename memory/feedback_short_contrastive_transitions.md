The user's own prose favours short contrastive transition adverbs — "Nevertheless," "Still," "Even so," "Yet" — placed at the start of a sentence, comma, then a direct clause. Example already in the paper: "Nevertheless, the coefficients should not be read individually."

**Why:** the user explicitly flagged this as a characteristic of their own writing during Results drafting for the trading-signal paper and asked me to "integrate it a little more" when proposing sentence options, rather than defaulting to longer contrastive constructions (e.g. "This does not mean...", "That said, ...", embedded "however").

**How to apply:** when a sentence's job is to pivot against what was just said, prefer offering at least one option that opens with a short contrastive adverb in this style, varying the specific word so it doesn't repeat the same one in adjacent paragraphs.
