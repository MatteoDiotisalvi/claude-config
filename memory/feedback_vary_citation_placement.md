Parenthetical citations (Author, Year) don't have to sit at the very end of every sentence. Attach the citation to the specific clause it actually supports — mid-sentence, right after the claim it backs — rather than defaulting to a mechanical "claim, claim, claim (Citation, Year)." pattern repeated across every sentence in a section.

**Why:** Said explicitly while drafting Related Work for [[the-verifier-that-cannot-see]], after several consecutive sentence options all ended on a trailing citation — the repetition itself was flagged as a formulaic, AI-typical pattern even though each individual sentence was fine on its own.

**How to apply:** When offering sentence options in a citation-dense section, deliberately mix placement across the three options offered — at least one with the citation attached mid-sentence to its supporting clause, not just at the end. Also fine to have a whole sentence run citation-free (a bridge, a scoping statement, a claim already covered by an adjacent citation) rather than forcing one into every sentence — this matches how real peer-reviewed Related Work sections read (confirmed against Huang et al., ICLR 2024, where roughly half the sentences carry no citation at all).
