Matteo has a separate, already-completed empirical research paper on nearly the same topic as his EE ("The impact of AI-derived news sentiment on signal generation for systematic trading", dated 18/08/2026, at `OneDrive\research project\full pdf submission.pdf`) that he intends to get published. It contains original empirical work (FinBERT sentiment scoring across 384 S&P 500 stocks, three backtested trading strategies with real regression/backtest results) that is a strong, citable evidence source for the EE — notably it's exactly the kind of out-of-sample, cost-adjusted, multi-regime test the EE's own Discussion section flags as missing from the existing literature.

**Rule:** when drafting or editing the EE, never reuse sentences or close paraphrases from this paper (or from the old pre-narrowing EE draft, which is oddly concatenated into the same PDF and already shares near-identical sentences with the live EE, e.g. the "systematic trading can be defined as..." (Bloch, 2025) definition). Cite the paper as an external source (e.g. "Diotisalvi (2026), unpublished manuscript") when drawing on its findings/data, and always write fresh prose for the EE rather than merging text across the two documents.

**Why:** confirmed via IB's own academic honesty policy (2026-09-05 web research) that self-plagiarism — reusing your own prior-assignment text without citation — is treated by the IB identically to any other plagiarism: EEs are run through Turnitin, and a flagged match can zero the EE grade and block the Diploma. Since Matteo wants the research paper published, keeping the two documents textually independent also protects the paper's own originality. See [[project_extended_essay]] for the broader EE conversion this constraint applies to.

**How to apply:** whenever the EE conversion work draws on the research paper (data, findings, methodology descriptions, or definitional sentences like "systematic trading is..."), paraphrase independently from scratch and add a citation rather than lifting wording, even when the underlying fact is the same.

**Related, separate requirement found 2026-09-05:** IB also requires any generative AI use in producing the EE to be acknowledged like any other source (confirmed via web search of current IB guidance) — the IB sets the floor, the supervisor decides the exact disclosure format (acknowledgements page, process journal, or RPPF mention), so ask the supervisor directly rather than assuming. See the master plan (`EE_CS_Master_Conversion_Plan.md`, rule A.2 and open decision F.6) for where this sits in the conversion workflow.

**Escalation (2026-09-05): decided not to cite the paper at all, pending supervisor input.** When actually drafting the Comparative Evidence section, a deeper problem surfaced than simple text-reuse: citing the paper as "(Diotisalvi, 2026, unpublished manuscript)" would ask an examiner to trust unverifiable empirical claims from a non-peer-reviewed source written by the candidate himself — unlike Tetlock/Bollen/Yang, it isn't independently checkable. It may also have had input from a mentor (Lumiere programme materials found in Matteo's research folder), which would need its own disclosure if presented as primary research instead of a citation. Matteo decided to write the EE without it for now, and only revisit once he's asked his supervisor directly which framing (cite as literature / disclose as primary research / don't use) is appropriate. Do not reintroduce this citation into any EE section without that confirmation.
